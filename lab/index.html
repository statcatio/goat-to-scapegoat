The Lab · Plates I–V

Five ways to draw the same argument

Before the piece found its shape, it was five different pieces. These are the working prototypes — kept as they were the day they were set aside, including the ones that lost.

Each plate below opens the real file. Two of them are the direct ancestors of what the finished article does; one contributed only its words; two were dead ends worth keeping.

Note These plates are frozen at the moment they were set aside, so the numbers on them are the placeholder values the piece was designed against. The finished article now runs on 28,051 real scored comments; these do not. They are kept for their form, not their figures. The only thing added since is a link back to this page in the corner of each.

Plate I

The almanac hero→

Copy survived

The first landing screen: a broadsheet masthead, a single sentiment gauge running −1 to +1 across both finals, and a Scrape → Analyze → Visualize method strip. The wordmark and the “did they change, or did we?” line were right on the first try. The layout around them was not — it read as a dashboard, and the piece needed to read as an essay.

Static · hand-rolled CSS

 Plate II

Theme flow, clicked→

Set aside

Five recurring themes in the discourse, each with its share of the conversation and its tone in 2022 versus 2026. Navigated by clicking a theme to bring it forward. It worked, and nobody clicked — which is the whole argument for the plate that follows.

Interactive · no library

 Plate III

Theme flow, scrolled→

Shipped

The same five themes, but the scroll position drives the highlight: step cards on one side, the flow diagram holding still on the other. Passive readers get the whole sequence for free. This mechanic is what the finished article’s “Same topics, opposite verdict” section became.

Scrollytelling · IntersectionObserver

 Plate IV

Theme flow, rebuilt in D3→

Set aside

The theme diagram redrawn with D3 scales and transitions, to see whether the library bought anything the hand-rolled version didn’t. For five fixed categories it bought about 280 KB and no new expressiveness. Kept as the receipt for that decision.

D3 v7 · inlined

 Plate V

Ninety minutes, two verdicts→

Shipped

The centerpiece: sentiment plotted minute by minute across both finals, stacked so the same match clock runs down two panels at once. Here D3 earned its weight — continuous time, event annotations, and a shared hover that has to hit the right comment in two datasets. This became the article’s timeline.

D3 v7 · linked panels

These files are frozen snapshots, not maintained pages — they can be rough at small widths and they don’t share the article’s polish. That is the point of keeping them.
